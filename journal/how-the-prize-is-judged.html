The Prize · Guide

How the Prime Art Prize is judged, step by step

An entry fee buys you one thing: a fair reading by people who show art for a living. This is how we make sure that is what you get.

The editors · 6 min read

1. The jury is named before you pay

Every round has three jurors: a gallery director, a critic and a curator. Their names and affiliations appear on our home page before the call opens, so you know who will look at your work before you decide to enter. If a juror has to step down, we name the replacement and tell every artist who has already entered.

Jurors receive a fixed honorarium agreed before the round. It does not change with the number of entries, so no juror has a reason to want more entries or particular ones.

2. The organizer does not vote

The Prime Art runs the call, checks that entries are complete and publishes the results. We do not score entries, we do not sit in on scoring, and we cannot move an artist onto or off the shortlist. Buying one of our paid services has no effect on judging, and jurors are not told who has bought one.

3. Eligibility check

Before judging starts, we check each entry against the published rules: the artist is 18 or over, the works were made within the last five years, and the images and details are complete. If something is missing we write to you once and give you time to fix it. We only remove an entry that still does not meet the rules after that.

4. Independent first reading

Each juror reviews every eligible entry online, on their own, without seeing the other jurors' scores. Entries are shown with images, titles, dates, media, dimensions and your short statement. Jurors score each entry on three things:

  • The work itself: how fully the work realises what it sets out to do.
  • A distinct voice: whether the work would be recognisable as yours.
  • Coherence of the entry: how the works you chose read together.

Career stage, exhibition history, nationality and the number of works entered (one, two or three) are not scored.

5. Conflicts of interest

A juror must abstain from any entry by an artist they represent, have curated, have written about for payment, teach, or are related to. Abstentions are recorded, and the abstaining juror's score for that entry is replaced by the average of the other two, so the artist is neither helped nor harmed.

6. Shortlist and winner

Scores are combined to produce a long list. The jury then meets online to agree the shortlist of twenty artists and the winner. Discussion can move an entry up or down, but the final decision belongs to the jury alone and is recorded with the date of the meeting.

7. What we publish afterwards

When results are announced we publish the number of entries received, the number of countries represented and the selection rate for the shortlist. Every shortlisted artist gets an English artist page and a place in the online exhibition at no further cost. The winner receives ₩3,000,000, a commissioned 1,000-word essay by an independent critic and a long-form interview.

If you think something went wrong with your entry, write to us. We answer every query about process, though we cannot reopen the jury's decision on the work.

The full conditions are in the rules of entry.
