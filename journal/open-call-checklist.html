Before you pay an entry fee: ten questions to ask any open call

Entry fees are normal for prizes and juried calls. What matters is what the fee pays for. These questions apply to every call, including ours.

The editors · 5 min read
  1. Who is on the jury, by name? A call that will not name its jurors before you pay is asking you to trust it blindly. Look the jurors up. Do they show, write about or collect work like yours?
  2. Who decides? Check whether the organizer also votes. If the people who take the fee also choose the winners, ask how that is kept fair.
  3. Is there anything to pay after selection? Hanging fees, catalogue fees, "exhibition contributions" and compulsory framing or shipping through the organizer are signs that selection is the start of the costs, not the end.
  4. What exactly do winners and finalists receive? Cash amounts should be stated in a currency, with how and when they are paid. "Exposure", "promotion" and "opportunities" should be described in concrete terms.
  5. Are the dates published? There should be a deadline, a judging period and a results date. Calls that keep extending their deadline may have too few entries to judge meaningfully.
  6. What rights do you give away? You should keep your copyright. A fair call asks only for permission to show images of your work to promote the call and the results, with your name credited.
  7. What happens if the call is cancelled? The rules should say whether your fee is refunded.
  8. Does the call publish its numbers? Entries received and the selection rate tell you how competitive it is and whether the organizer is open about how it works.
  9. Can you find past winners? If the call has run before, look up its past winners and shortlists. Are they artists whose careers you recognise or would want to follow?
  10. Is there a real person to write to? Send a question before you enter. How quickly and clearly the organizer replies is often the best guide to how your entry will be handled.

How we answer these

For the Prime Art Prize: the three jurors are named before the call opens; the organizer does not vote; there are no fees after selection; the winner receives ₩3,000,000, a commissioned essay and an interview; you keep your copyright; the entry fee is refunded in full if the round is cancelled; and we publish entries, countries and the selection rate after every round. Details are in the rules of entry and in how judging works.
